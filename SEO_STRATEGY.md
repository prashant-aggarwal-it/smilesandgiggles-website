# SEO Strategy & Search Launch Plan

This release treats Smiles & Giggles Foundation as a multi-theme education and community NGO rather than a STEM-only site. The site should communicate the real work clearly to people first; search terms are mapped to the page that best answers that intent instead of being repeated across every page.

## Core search themes

- STEM education and hands-on learning
- Robotics education and physical computing
- Coding and computational thinking
- AI literacy and responsible AI use
- Digital literacy and practical digital skills
- Future-ready skills and youth skilling
- Government-school education programmes
- Teacher enablement and capacity building
- CSR education / STEM / robotics / digital-skills programmes
- Preventive health and oral-health awareness
- Delhi / Delhi NCR organisational intent where geographically relevant

## On-page rules

1. Keep one clear primary topic per page. Do not paste a long keyword list into body copy.
2. Use natural language in titles, H1/H2 headings, introductions, internal links and image alt text only where it accurately describes the content or image.
3. Keep all impact claims evidence-led. Search optimisation must never create or imply programme results that are not documented.
4. Maintain descriptive internal links between related programme, impact and CSR pages.
5. Continue using canonical URLs, production-only indexing, sitemap, robots rules, Open Graph/Twitter metadata and structured data.
6. Do not add a meta-keywords tag; it is intentionally omitted.

## Post-launch search work

1. Verify the domain in Google Search Console and submit `/sitemap.xml`.
2. Inspect and request indexing for the homepage, Our Work, the three programme pages, Impact and CSR Partnerships.
3. Review Search Console queries monthly. Use actual impressions/clicks to decide which pages need clearer content or new field stories.
4. If eligible for a public-facing location profile, claim and maintain the Google Business Profile with the same name, address and phone used on the website.
5. Publish authentic field stories/case studies when evidence is available. Prefer programme name, location/context, activities, documented participation and approved photographs over generic SEO articles.
6. Seek legitimate citations/links from partner schools, institutional collaborators, CSR partners, event pages and credible media or sector organisations. Do not buy links or mass-submit to low-quality directories.
7. Monitor Core Web Vitals, mobile usability, indexing issues and rich-result/schema errors in Search Console/PageSpeed.

## Content opportunities after launch

Only publish these when there is real, approved evidence behind them:

- Government-school STEM / robotics programme field story
- Student project demonstration or showcase story
- Teacher enablement workshop story
- AI literacy or digital-skills cohort story
- CSR partnership implementation case study
- Preventive oral-health awareness / screening initiative story

A useful field story should state what was delivered, who it was for, what learners did, what evidence was documented, and what can responsibly be said about progress. Avoid guaranteed-outcome language.
